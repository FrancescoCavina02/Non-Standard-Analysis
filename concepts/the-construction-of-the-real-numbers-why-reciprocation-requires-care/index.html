The construction of the real numbers / Remark

Why reciprocation requires care

Formal statement

Suppose that a real number is represented by a Cauchy sequence
(an)n=1∞(a_n)_{n=1}^{\infty}(an​)n=1∞​, so that
x=LIM⁡n→∞an. x=\operatorname{LIM}_{n\to\infty}a_n. x=LIMn→∞​an​.
A natural first attempt is to define its reciprocal term by term:
x−1:=LIM⁡n→∞an−1. x^{-1} := \operatorname{LIM}_{n\to\infty}a_n^{-1}. x−1:=LIMn→∞​an−1​.
However, this definition does not always make sense.

For example, the Cauchy sequence
0.1,  0.01,  0.001,  0.0001,… 0.1,\;0.01,\;0.001,\;0.0001,\ldots 0.1,0.01,0.001,0.0001,…
represents the real number 000. Its termwise reciprocal is
10,  100,  1000,  10000,…, 10,\;100,\;1000,\;10000,\ldots, 10,100,1000,10000,…,
which is unbounded and therefore is not a Cauchy sequence. This reflects
the familiar fact that 000 has no reciprocal.

There is also a more subtle problem. A non-zero real number can be
represented by a Cauchy sequence containing some zero terms. For
instance, the sequence
0,  0.9,  0.99,  0.999,… 0,\;0.9,\;0.99,\;0.999,\ldots 0,0.9,0.99,0.999,…
represents the real number 111, but its first term cannot be inverted.
Thus, even when x≠0x\neq 0x=0, reciprocation cannot necessarily be applied
to every term of an arbitrary sequence representing xxx.

The essential idea is that, when x≠0x\neq 0x=0, one must choose or modify
the representing Cauchy sequence so that its terms are eventually
bounded away from zero. Once there exists a positive rational number
